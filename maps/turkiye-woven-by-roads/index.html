Türkiye, Woven by Roads

Can a road network make a country recognisable? In the flat map, Türkiye’s outline emerges from the linework itself. In the 3D view, the same three road classes sit on the surface of an extruded national outline. The two views let you compare the shape suggested by the roads with the shape defined by the boundary.

The data behind the map

I prepared the road network in QGIS from a Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team export. Red shows motorways and trunk roads, blue shows primary through tertiary roads, and gray shows local roads and tracks. The tiles generalise the network at national scale. For the Blender rendering, I used the geoBoundaries national outline for the base and clipped all three road layers to that same geometry.

Reading the network at two scales

At national scale, the red lines make it easier to follow long corridors, while the finer roads show where the network branches. The Bosphorus close-up uses higher-detail road data. It brings out the crossings between the two sides and the smaller streets that feed into the main routes.

Roads make connections possible, but the number of lines in an area tells only part of the story. A short link can matter when it connects two large parts of a network, while a dense group of local streets may mostly serve nearby trips. The map shows road classes and their spatial arrangement. A study of accessibility would also need travel times, crossing points and the locations of destinations such as jobs or hospitals.
