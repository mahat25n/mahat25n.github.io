Where the Tracks Divide (animation)

Two İstanbul high-speed services follow the same western line before separating near Polatlı. The Sivas service continues through Ankara, while the Karaman service turns south toward Konya. The second part of the video follows the main road network region by region. That change of scale makes it possible to compare a pair of intercity rail services with the roads that carry people and goods between many more places. TCDD Taşımacılık high-speed services.

What a connection buys

When trains share a corridor, they can use the same track and stations to serve different destinations. Demand along the common stretch helps support more departures, provided that the timetable has room for them. The service is valuable to passengers if they can reach the station, catch a train at a useful time and complete the trip at a fare they can afford. A road or bus connection at either end can determine whether a new rail service makes a job, meeting or family visit easier to reach.

For freight, the connection that matters may be a short link between a factory or port and a conventional rail terminal. A lorry can do that first part of the trip, with the railway carrying the load over a longer distance. The moving lights trace map geometry and service routes.
