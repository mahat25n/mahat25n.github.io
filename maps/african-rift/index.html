Where Africa is splitting apart

The physical geography of a continent sets conditions within which economic decisions are made. Relief affects the routes available to roads and other infrastructure, while tectonic settings raise questions about hazards and natural resources. Those questions require care because a physical feature is not an economic outcome.

The data behind the map

I combined NOAA ETOPO 2022 elevation and bathymetry with PB2002 plate boundaries and Natural Earth geographic layers. Shaded relief helps the reader see changes in terrain, while the marked lines identify boundaries from the plate model. Python was used to prepare and render the layers. The image describes a tectonic setting; it does not forecast the timing of a future geological event.

Terrain, infrastructure and risk

One question for infrastructure planning is the cost of connecting settlements across uneven terrain. Another is how long-lived infrastructure accounts for hazards. A plate boundary, however, is not a local hazard probability, and elevation alone cannot establish construction costs. Both applications require engineering and site-specific information beyond the map.

The title refers to a geological process, not an imminent division of the continent or its economies. Treating the image as a forecast would obscure its more useful contribution: helping readers locate the physical systems that economic planning must investigate. Geography frames the questions, while institutions, technology and investment choices shape the responses.
