How clear is the ocean?

Ocean conditions matter to economic activities that share the same coastal space. Fishing, tourism, shipping and coastal development use marine environments in different ways. Before discussing their costs or benefits, it helps to distinguish what a satellite can observe from what an economic assessment would need to know.

The data behind the map

This map uses ESA Ocean Colour CCI version 6.0, accessed through NOAA OceanWatch, for the monthly September 2026 field. The mapped variable is Kd490, the diffuse attenuation coefficient at a wavelength of 490 nanometres. It describes how quickly light diminishes with depth. Lower values indicate that light penetrates farther into the water; missing observations remain separate from the colour classes.

From ocean conditions to livelihoods

The Equal Earth projection supports comparison across the global map. Coastal differences invite questions about sediment, biological activity and the processes that affect light penetration, but the colours do not identify their causes. Clearer water is not automatically healthier water, and high attenuation is not a direct measure of pollution or economic damage.

An economic application would connect changes in this physical measure to a particular activity and time period. For example, assessing a coastal intervention would require observations before and after it, a credible comparison and evidence on the affected resource or service. The monthly image supplies environmental context for such an evaluation. A claim about livelihoods would require evidence on the people and activities affected.
