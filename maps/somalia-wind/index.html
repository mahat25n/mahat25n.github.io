The wind that could power Somalia

A renewable-energy resource becomes an economic asset only when it can be converted into reliable, affordable power. Somalia’s wind resource invites that discussion, but a resource map and an investment appraisal answer different questions.

The data behind the map

The colours show modelled mean wind speed at 100 metres above ground from Global Wind Atlas 3. Terrain information from AWS Terrain Tiles provides geographic context, with Natural Earth supplying the base geography. Python was used to prepare and combine these layers. The image represents wind conditions at a stated height, not electricity already generated.

From wind resource to delivered power

A windy location is one input into a project decision. Transmission distance, access, maintenance, financing and the timing of electricity demand also affect its value. Mean wind speed does not describe all the variation over a day or year, and it cannot be converted directly into output without specifying a turbine and its operating characteristics.

The economic question is therefore where a resource could meet demand at an acceptable delivered cost. For communities and businesses, reliability matters alongside price. Investment choices would need site measurements, network information and a realistic account of who would purchase the power. The map gives that conversation a geographic starting point; it does not rank projects or promise a particular amount of generation.
