Atlas of Scent

A perfume can bring several places into the same bottle. This map starts with five places associated with distinct scents, from May rose in Grasse to vanilla in Madagascar’s Sava region.

The data behind the map

I placed five reference points in QGIS. The map pairs Grasse, France, with May rose, Reggio Calabria, Italy, with bergamot, Dhofar, Oman, with frankincense, Kannauj, India, with mitti attar, and Sava, Madagascar, with vanilla. I used Blender to render the map and draw the coloured trails. The points refer to the named places or regions. The trails are illustrative.

Scent and place

The five examples connect scent to different kinds of places, from cities associated with perfumery to regions associated with cultivation and harvesting. Each point gives one geographic reference for the scent. The map represents five selected associations and does not estimate where production is concentrated or how fragrances move through trade.

From harvest to perfume

Grasse offers one example of how a scent becomes associated with a place. UNESCO traces the cultivation and processing of perfume plants in the area back to at least the sixteenth century, when leather tanning was a major local trade. Its 2018 heritage listing recognises the work of growers, processors and perfume composers. The May rose on the map represents one part of a local craft that depends on people knowing how to grow flowers, extract their fragrance and combine it with other materials. Caravans, ports and export markets

In Dhofar, the geography of frankincense included both the places where trees grew and the places where resin changed hands. Wadi Dawkah preserves frankincense trees. Shisr was an inland caravan stop, and the ports of Khor Rori and Al-Baleed connected the region to maritime trade in different periods. These sites help explain how an aromatic resin harvested in southern Oman reached markets far from where it grew. UNESCO’s Land of Frankincense ↗

Vanilla connects Sava to a more recent export economy. The World Bank identifies the region as the source of most Malagasy vanilla and documents sharp swings in vanilla prices. For growers, the place name on a product label is also a place where harvests and prices affect farm incomes. Mapping Sava alongside Dhofar and Grasse brings these different relationships into view. A scent can be associated with a farming region, a trade route or a set of skills that people pass on through practice. World Bank study of Malagasy trade ↗ · World Bank analysis of vanilla prices ↗
