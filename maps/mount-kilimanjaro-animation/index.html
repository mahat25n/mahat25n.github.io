Mount Kilimanjaro, Africa’s Highest Peak (animation)

The flyover begins with Africa, locates Tanzania on the continent and then moves to the relief of Mount Kilimanjaro. Uhuru Peak, on Kibo, reaches 5,895 metres and is Africa’s highest point. As the camera turns, the relief shows why distance on a flat map tells only part of the story of reaching the summit. Forest, moorland and higher alpine ground succeed one another over a steep ascent. UNESCO World Heritage Centre.

What the climb supports

A multi-day ascent requires guides, porters, cooks and equipment before a party takes its first step. In towns near the park, the same visitors can buy rooms, transport and food. Where an operator buys its supplies and how it pays its crew determine how far that demand reaches into the local economy. A visitor who adds time in the region has more opportunities to buy directly from local businesses than one who goes straight from the airport to the park and back.

Managing the climb also costs money. Park staff need to keep trails and camps in use, manage waste and prepare for rescue at altitude. The relief is useful here because it shows what cannot be understood from an arrival count alone. Visits concentrate along routes and at certain elevations. Those places bear much of the cost of use, while protecting the forested slopes benefits households and farms below the park. Read the full essay on Kilimanjaro and Tanzania’s tourism economy.
