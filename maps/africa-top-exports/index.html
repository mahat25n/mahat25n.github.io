What Africa exports

A country’s leading export is a useful introduction to its trading economy, but it is an incomplete account of how that economy earns income. This map asks a deliberately narrow question: which product generates the largest recorded export value in each African country?

The data behind the map

The data come from CEPII’s BACI bilateral merchandise-trade database. I aggregated 2024 HS92 six-digit product records from release V202601 into four-digit headings, excluding chapters 88 and 89, which cover aircraft, spacecraft, ships and associated items. Country colours identify the leading product category. They do not measure export value, trade growth or the contribution of exports to household income.

Exports, employment and exposure

A dominant product can connect a country to a large international market while exposing it to changes in the price of that product. The consequences depend on more than its position in a ranking. Economists would also examine its share of total exports, domestic value added, employment and the destination of export receipts. A capital-intensive activity and a labour-intensive activity can produce similar export revenues with very different effects on employment.

The map offers a starting point for a discussion of diversification. Diversification is not simply a matter of adding more product names to a list. It concerns the capabilities, transport links and institutions that allow firms to compete in additional markets. Services and production for domestic consumers are outside this merchandise-export picture, so the colours should never be mistaken for a complete description of national economic activity.
