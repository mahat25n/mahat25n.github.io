Global markets after the tariff announcement

A policy announcement changes markets through expectations. Investors reassess future earnings, costs and risk before the eventual effects on production and trade can be observed. A geographic animation allows those adjustments to be compared across financial centres without treating the world as a single trading session.

The data behind the map

This animation uses Yahoo Finance daily closing levels for selected stock indices from 26 March to 24 April 2025. Each market is compared with its own local close on 2 April, before the US tariff announcement. Coral indicates a level below that baseline and teal a level above it. Python prepares the series and renders the animation on an Equal Earth map.

Prices, expectations and attribution

The observations are in local currency and follow local trading calendars. They are not simultaneous global prices or returns converted into one investor’s currency. Where a market is closed, the display retains its last available observation. Index construction also differs, so the series should not be assumed to have identical dividend treatment.

This is evidence of repricing around an event, not proof of a single cause. Exchange rates, monetary expectations and other news can move alongside tariff announcements. Country shading represents the selected market index, not the performance of every firm or the whole national economy. The animation makes the sequence visible; a causal assessment would require a separate design and evidence about firms’ exposure to the policy.
