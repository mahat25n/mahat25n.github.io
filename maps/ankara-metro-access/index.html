Day 20 · Hexagons · 2026 collection

How close is the metro in Ankara?

Within Ankara Province, proximity to rail changes sharply between the city’s central corridors and the outer districts. The map shows where that change happens across the same grid, even where a district boundary crosses a corridor.

The data behind the map

In QGIS, I measured the straight-line distance from the centre of each of 666 hexagons to the nearest of 47 station locations published by Sadati (2023). I clipped the grid to the boundary of Ankara Province and overlaid its 25 districts. The lightest class is under 2.5 km from a mapped station. Red dots show the stations. I rendered the map in Blender.

Proximity and access

The shortest distances follow the rail corridors near central Ankara. Much of the outer province falls in the longer distance bands. The measure describes geographic proximity to the published locations. Estimating walking access would require station entrances and a pedestrian network. The source also predates changes to the rail network since publication.

How Ankara’s rail corridors took shape

The Kızılay–Batıkent line opened in 1997. In 2014, the Batıkent–Sincan/Törekent and Kızılay–Çayyolu lines added connections farther west and southwest. That history helps explain why the closest hexagons form branches along particular corridors. Two places at a similar distance from Kızılay can fall into different bands depending on how close they are to one of those lines. EGO’s history of Ankara public transport ↗

Why access matters in daily life

A station offers a way into the network. What matters for daily life is whether that network gets people to work, school, shops or healthcare within the time and money they have. The same apartment can be well placed for a job near a direct rail line and poorly placed for a hospital that requires a bus transfer. The International Transport Forum measures accessibility by counting which destinations people can reach within a given time by each mode. That approach connects transport service to the opportunities people need. International Transport Forum’s accessibility framework ↗

Consider two homes half a kilometre from a station on this map. From one, a direct crossing might lead to the entrance. From the other, a divided road or a steep approach might lengthen the walk. Step-free entrances, operating lifts and reliable transfers can also change which trips are practical. A longer distance to a metro station may matter less where a frequent bus or commuter rail service makes the whole trip easier.

A next step for Ankara would be to calculate door-to-door travel times to jobs, clinics and schools using station entrances, footpaths and service timetables. Population data would show how many people live in areas with few reachable destinations. That would help distinguish places where a safer crossing would improve access from places where a better bus connection or more frequent service would do more.
