The age of the world’s temperature records

The capacity to measure climate has an economic geography of its own. Weather records support decisions about crops, infrastructure and insurance, but their usefulness depends partly on how far back they reach. This map turns attention from temperature itself to the history of its observation.

Reading the map

Station records come from NOAA’s Global Historical Climatology Network monthly dataset, GHCN-M version 4. Each station is coloured by the beginning of its temperature record. A double-hemisphere composition gives the map the character of an older atlas while retaining a clearly defined statistical subject: the recorded start of a station series.

The economic interpretation

An early start does not guarantee continuous observations, stable instruments or an unchanged site. The map also does not weight stations by the population or economic activity around them. It should therefore be read as an account of record beginnings, not as a league table of climate-data quality.

Long records can provide context for judging whether recent conditions are unusual. Where records are shorter or incomplete, uncertainty enters decisions that already involve long horizons. An economist would ask who bears that uncertainty and whether investment in observation has benefits beyond the institution collecting the data. The map makes the measurement infrastructure visible, without pretending to value those benefits or measure the accuracy of any particular climate forecast.
